# Core Argument

Command: `/theory`. Sources: [[Project]], [[Measurement Research Design]]. RESEARCHER MATERIAL, reorganized for navigation — no new theoretical content added.

## The Argument in One Paragraph

Political alignment among national governments, local authorities, and displaced populations should not be treated as a uniform or scalar relationship. Its consequences for government behaviour depend on (1) whether alignment is **shared** across national and local authorities or **fragmented** between them, and (2) **where** meaningful authority over a given policy domain is institutionally located. Because both alignment and authority vary independently across domains and territories, governments can produce **heterogeneous response profiles** — protective in one domain, repressive in another — rather than a single uniform stance toward displaced populations.

## Why a Multilevel, Non-Scalar Argument

Three literatures motivate treating alignment as a *configuration* rather than a single number:

- **Politics of displacement** (Kalyvas, 2006; Steele, 2017) shows displacement itself can be a strategic instrument of territorial/political control — meaning the political relationship between state and displaced population is not incidental, but constitutive of state behaviour.
- **Multilevel governance** (Niedzwiecki, 2016) shows states are not unitary: incentives and implementation vary across territorial levels, and alignment *between* levels of government shapes policy outcomes independently of either level's alignment taken alone.
- **Local governance of displacement** (Weihmayer, 2024) shows local authorities can have a decisive, and potentially divergent, relationship with IDPs compared to the central state.

Put together: a two-actor (state–displaced population) or single-level (national only) model of alignment cannot represent the cases the project cares about, because it would collapse exactly the variation (shared vs. fragmented; national vs. local locus of authority) that the theory says matters.

## Core Theoretical Claim vs. Research Expectation

| | Statement |
|---|---|
| THEORETICAL CLAIM | Political alignment is a multilevel configuration (not a scalar), and its effect on government response is conditioned by the institutional location of authority over the relevant policy domain. |

## Position Relative to Existing GRID Framework

The argument explicitly builds on and extends GRID's own "Protect and Repress" framing (Steele, Schwartz, & Lichtenheld, 2024), which already treats government response along a protection–repression continuum shaped by political alignment. This project's specific contribution is disaggregating "alignment" into a multilevel configuration and disaggregating "response" into a multi-domain, de jure/de facto structure (see [[Political Alignment]], [[Protection]], [[Repression]]).
