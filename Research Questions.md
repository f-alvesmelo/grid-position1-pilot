# Research Questions

> Source: [[Project]]. RESEARCHER MATERIAL unless marked otherwise.

## Main Research Question

> "How do configurations of political alignment among national governments, local authorities and displaced populations shape the composition of government protection and repression across territories and policy domains?"

### Components of the question

| Element | As stated in the project |
|---|---|
| Explanandum (what varies) | Composition of government protection and repression |
| Explanans (proposed explanation) | Configurations of political alignment among national governments, local authorities, and displaced populations |
| Scope of variation | Across territories and across policy domains |

## Researcher Decisions (2026-09-27)

> Recorded in [[Changelog]]. These are RESEARCHER decisions resolving items previously flagged ⚠ OPERATIONALIZATION OPEN, not AGENT proposals.

- **Response composition is multi-domain**, not a single scalar. Government response is coded per policy domain, not as one aggregate protection–repression score.
- **De jure and de facto responses are coded separately** — formal policy and observed implementation/practice are treated as distinct variables per domain, not collapsed into one.
- **Pilot policy domain: humanitarian assistance.** This is the first (and, for this pilot, only) domain to be fully operationalized and coded. Per [[Measurement Research Design]], the illustrative full domain list is: humanitarian assistance, public services, rights and legal guarantees, mobility and territorial control, and other domains pending the finalized GRID framework.

## Sub-Questions

The project does not enumerate separate sub-research-questions distinct from the main question; the three hypotheses (below) operate as the effective sub-questions/expectations. The pilot bounds the general research question to a single domain (humanitarian assistance) so the design can be demonstrated end-to-end; the cross-domain and cross-territory scope of the original question remains the long-run ambition.

## Hypotheses / Expectations

> Distinguished per AGENT.md §9: these are RESEARCH EXPECTATIONS, not established findings.

**H1**
> Greater alignment among national governments, local authorities, and IDPs will be associated with more protective response profiles.

- Type: THEORETICAL CLAIM → RESEARCH EXPECTATION
- Implied DV: protective response profile (composition of protection)
- Implied IV: degree/sharedness of political alignment across levels

**H2**
> Fragmented political alignment is associated with more heterogeneous combinations of protective and repressive responses.

- Type: THEORETICAL CLAIM → RESEARCH EXPECTATION
- Implied DV: heterogeneity of response composition (mix of protection and repression)
- Implied IV: fragmentation (vs. shared alignment) across levels

**H3**
> The relationship between political alignment and government responses varies according to the institutional location of authority over the relevant policy domain.

- Type: THEORETICAL CLAIM → RESEARCH EXPECTATION (moderation/interaction hypothesis)
- Implied moderator: institutional location of authority over the policy domain
- This hypothesis makes H1/H2 conditional rather than universal — it is the theoretical payoff of treating alignment as multilevel rather than scalar.

## Dependent Variable(s) (as implied by the RQ, hypotheses, and researcher decisions above)

- **Government response**, per policy domain, decomposed into:
  - **De jure response** — formal law, policy, regulation, or institutional commitment (humanitarian assistance domain, for the pilot).
  - **De facto response** — observed implementation, enforcement, or practice (humanitarian assistance domain, for the pilot).
- Each is positioned along the protection–repression continuum, potentially varying by territory (cross-national and subnational) and, beyond the pilot, by domain.

✓ **RESOLVED, per [[Measurement Research Design]] §3, §6**: coding categories are *protective / repressive-restrictive / no formal response (de jure)* and *protective / repressive-restrictive / no meaningful state action (de facto)*, cross-tabulated into a 2×2 consistency matrix (consistent protection / protective-inconsistent / repressive-inconsistent / consistent repression-restriction). See [[Coding Protocol]] and [[Data Dictionary]].

## Independent Variable(s) (as implied by the RQ and hypotheses)

- **Political alignment configuration** — shared vs. fragmented, across national government, local authorities, and displaced populations.
- **Institutional location of authority** over the policy domain (moderator, per H3): national / local / shared / delegated.

✓ **RESOLVED, per [[Measurement Research Design]] §4–5**: alignment is measured **relationally and categorically**, not as a single scalar. Three dyads are coded separately (national government↔local authority; national government↔displaced population; local authority↔displaced population), each as aligned (2) / cross-cutting-ambiguous (1) / opposed (0) / insufficient evidence (NA). The displaced population is treated as a *specific, politically salient group tied to the displacement episode* (not assumed homogeneous), measured in the pre-displacement/onset period to avoid post-treatment bias. See [[Political Alignment]] and [[Coding Protocol]].

## Units of Analysis

- Cross-national: **country-year**, or possibly **country-year-displacement-episode** — per [[Measurement Research Design]] §7, the exact unit is intentionally left open pending inspection of the finalized GRID dataset structure. [TO VERIFY] once that inspection happens.
- Colombia: **municipality-year** (proposed candidate in-depth case, not a fixed requirement).
- Iraq: **location-level** (displacement/return data) — case-study context, not part of the cross-national country universe.
- Burundi: case-based, with more limited quantitative measures — case-study context, not part of the cross-national country universe.
