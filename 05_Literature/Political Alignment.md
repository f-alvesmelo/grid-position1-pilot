# Political Alignment (Literature)

Command: `/literature`. Sources: [[Project]]. See [[Literature Map]] for the full citation table. For the *operationalization* of political alignment (as opposed to its literature grounding), see [[Political Alignment]] in `01_Theory/`.

## Multilevel Governance

Niedzwiecki (2016) shows that states are not unitary actors: political incentives and policy implementation can vary across territorial levels, and political alignment *between* national and subnational governments can independently affect policy outcomes, using a subnational comparison of Argentina and Brazil (social policy domain, not displacement).

**Relevance**: this is the direct literature source for the project's central theoretical move — treating alignment as a multilevel configuration rather than a single national-level attribute. The project explicitly extends this logic from social policy to internal displacement response.

## Local Governance of Displacement

Weihmayer (2024) demonstrates, for Colombia specifically, that local authorities can play a decisive role in access to services, political participation, and durable solutions for IDPs, and that IDP–local-government relationships may develop differently from IDP–central-state relationships.

**Relevance**: this is the direct literature source for treating the **local authority ↔ displaced population** dyad as analytically distinct from the **national government ↔ displaced population** dyad — i.e., the theoretical justification for the three-dyad structure in [[Political Alignment]] (`01_Theory/`).

## The GRID Framework's Own Alignment Concept

Steele, Schwartz, & Lichtenheld (2024) already propose that political alignment among multi-level actors shapes government responses (see [[Government Responses]]). This project's specific contribution, relative to that framework, is to specify *how* alignment should be measured at the multilevel/configurational level — which neither Niedzwiecki (2016) nor Weihmayer (2024) nor the GRID framing paper itself does explicitly, per the project text. That specification is supplied by the researcher's own subsequent memos, not by cited literature (see [[Measurement Research Design]], [[Refined Measurement Framework]]).

## What Remains a Literature Gap

None of the three sources above jointly theorize a **three-actor** (national government, local authority, displaced population) alignment configuration in a single framework — each source covers at most two of the three relationships (Niedzwiecki: national–local; Weihmayer: local–displaced population). The project's combination of all three dyads into one configuration is, per the project text, the researcher's own theoretical synthesis, not a direct restatement of any single cited source. See [[Research Gaps]].
