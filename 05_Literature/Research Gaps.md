# Research Gaps

Command: `/literature`. Synthesizes gaps already identified across [[Internal Displacement]], [[Government Responses]], [[Protection and Repression]], and [[Political Alignment]] (literature). The core gap statement below is RESEARCHER MATERIAL, quoted from [[Project]]; the itemized sub-gaps are the agent's organization of material already present across the literature files — not new claims.

## The Core Gap (as stated by the researcher)

> "We know considerably less about the political relationships that explain why governments respond to displaced populations with protection in some circumstances and repression or exclusion in others."

This sits at the intersection of three literatures that, per [[Project]], "have largely developed separately":

1. **Politics of displacement** (Kalyvas 2006; Steele 2011, 2017) — strategic production of displacement, not government response to it.
2. **Multilevel governance** (Niedzwiecki 2016) — political alignment across levels shapes policy outcomes, but not tested for displacement response.
3. **Local governance of displacement** (Weihmayer 2024) — local authorities matter for IDPs, but not integrated with national-level alignment or with a repression/protection outcome frame.

## Itemized Gaps

| Gap | Which literature leaves it open | This project's response |
|---|---|---|
| No literature jointly theorizes a three-actor (national, local, displaced population) alignment *configuration* | Niedzwiecki (2016) covers national–local only; Weihmayer (2024) covers local–displaced population only | Combines both into a single multilevel configuration — see [[Political Alignment]] (theory) |
| Protection–repression continuum has a thin, single-source literature base (Steele/Schwartz/Lichtenheld 2024 only) | No independently-authored theoretical literature on this specific continuum is cited | Flagged honestly in [[Protection and Repression]] rather than papered over with invented citations |
| No measurement instrument for multilevel political alignment exists in the cited literature | Niedzwiecki (2016), Weihmayer (2024), and the GRID framing paper (2024) do not specify a measurement instrument | Supplied by the researcher's own subsequent memos ([[Measurement Research Design]], [[Refined Measurement Framework]]), not by literature — this is researcher-original operationalization work |
| Existing Colombia literature (Steele 2011, 2017) studies the *production* of displacement, not government response *after* displacement | Explicitly acknowledged by the researcher in [[Project]] | Colombia is retained as a case but with an explicitly different research question (see [[Case Selection]]) |
| De jure/de facto divergence is documented (Lichtenheld & Steele 2026) but not previously built into a systematic coding instrument for a protection/repression outcome | Lichtenheld & Steele (2026) documents the pattern descriptively | Formalized into `response_de_jure`/`response_de_facto` variables and a consistency matrix (see [[Data Dictionary]], [[Coding Protocol]]) |
| No literature establishes whether the *displaced population's* political alignment (as opposed to conflict/citizenship dynamics) independently predicts response | Kalyvas (2006), Steele (2011, 2017), Tarkhanova (2023) address related but distinct mechanisms (violence strategy, political cleansing, citizenship/status) | This is the project's central empirical question (H1–H3) |

## What This Means for the Pilot

The pilot's job is not to fill all of these gaps — only to demonstrate that the researcher can move from an identified gap to a falsifiable design (see [[Mechanisms]], [[Observable Implications]]). Per AGENT.md §26, the agent has not filled any of these gaps with invented literature; where the project text does not cite a relevant source, this file says so rather than manufacturing one.
