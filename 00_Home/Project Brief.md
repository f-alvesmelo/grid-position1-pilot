# Project Brief

> Source: [[Project]] (researcher's original project statement). This brief restates and organizes RESEARCHER MATERIAL only. No claims, evidence, or findings have been added by the agent.

## Working Title

A Multilevel Analysis of Government Responses to Internal Displacement

## Research Puzzle

Existing scholarship explains a great deal about *why displacement happens* — including the strategic use of displacement by state and non-state actors (Kalyvas, 2006; Steele, 2017) — and increasingly about the formal policies states adopt *after* displacement occurs (Lichtenheld & Steele, 2026). What remains underexplored is the political relationships that explain **why governments respond to displaced populations with protection in some circumstances and repression or exclusion in others.**

## Research Question

> "How do configurations of political alignment among national governments, local authorities and displaced populations shape the composition of government protection and repression across territories and policy domains?"

See [[Research Questions]] for the full breakdown of the question, sub-elements, and hypotheses.

## Theoretical Argument

The project builds on the GRID project's "Protect and Repress" framework, which conceptualizes government responses to internal displacement along a **protection–repression continuum** and proposes that **political alignment among multi-level actors** shapes these responses (Steele, Schwartz, & Lichtenheld, 2024).

The central theoretical move (RESEARCHER MATERIAL): political alignment should **not** be treated as a uniform or scalar relationship. Its consequences depend on:

1. whether alignment is **shared** across national and local authorities or **fragmented** between them, and
2. **where** meaningful authority over a particular policy domain is institutionally located.

This is why governments can produce **heterogeneous response profiles** across territories and policy domains, rather than a single uniform response.

## Literatures Integrated

The project explicitly connects three literatures that have "largely developed separately":

1. **Politics of displacement** — state/non-state actors strategically use displacement to reshape territorial and political relations (Kalyvas, 2006; Steele, 2017).
2. **Multilevel governance** — states are not unitary actors; political incentives and implementation vary across territorial levels, and alignment between national/subnational governments affects policy outcomes (Niedzwiecki, 2016).
3. **Local governance of internal displacement** — local authorities play a decisive role in access to services, political participation, and durable solutions; IDP–local government relations may diverge from IDP–central state relations (Weihmayer, 2024).

## Core Concepts

- **Political alignment** — configuration of political relationships among national governments, local authorities, and displaced populations (multilevel, not scalar). See [[Political Alignment]].
- **Protection** and **Repression** — the two poles of the government-response continuum. See [[Protection]] and [[Repression]].
- **Institutional location of authority** — where formal/effective authority over a given policy domain sits within the multilevel system.
- Implicit in the framing (RESEARCHER MATERIAL, not yet formalized as a variable): the distinction between **formal policy** and **implementation/practice**, i.e., de jure vs. de facto responses ("states adopt formal policies toward IDPs, but implementation varies... informal practices may diverge from formal commitments").

## Hypotheses / Expectations

- **H1** — Greater alignment among national governments, local authorities, and IDPs is associated with more protective response profiles.
- **H2** — Fragmented political alignment is associated with more heterogeneous combinations of protective and repressive responses.
- **H3** — The relationship between political alignment and government responses varies according to the institutional location of authority over the relevant policy domain.

## Methodological Design (Nested, Mixed-Method)

An integrated nested design (Lieberman, 2005; Seawright, 2016; Beach & Pedersen, 2019) combining:

1. **Cross-national statistical analysis** of the GRID dataset of government responses to internal displacement (panel models with country/temporal fixed effects; event-study specifications around identifiable changes in alignment; R-based pipeline: harmonisation → estimation → clustered inference → reproducible visualisation).
2. **Subnational quantitative analysis — Colombia** as the principal case: a municipality-year dataset linking the Registro Único de Víctimas with municipal electoral/political data and conflict indicators; electoral turnover as a potential source of temporal variation for event-study analysis.
3. **Comparative qualitative/mixed evidence — Iraq and Burundi**, treated as complementary rather than statistically equivalent cases (asymmetric nested design): Iraq offers location-level displacement/return data and institutional/territorial-authority variation; Burundi offers a contrasting political context with more limited quantitative alignment measures. Process tracing (Beach & Pedersen, 2019; Bennett & Checkel, 2015) using government/administrative documents, policy records, humanitarian sources, and interviews where feasible, distinguishing political explanations from competing accounts based on (i) administrative capacity, (ii) conflict intensity, and (iii) territorial control.

## Identification Strategy (as stated by researcher)

The researcher explicitly flags a key identification concern: political alignment may affect not only government responses but also the **production and composition of displacement itself** (reverse causality / confounding). Proposed mitigations (RESEARCHER MATERIAL):

- Distinguish pre-displacement political alignment from subsequent government responses.
- Distinguish displacement caused by the responding state from displacement it did not cause.
- Lagged measures, alternative operationalisations, theoretically justified covariates.
- Robustness and sensitivity analyses.
- Treat measurement validity as a central methodological concern, since political alignment can take different institutional forms across contexts (Adcock & Collier, 2001).

## Contribution (as stated by researcher)

- **Theoretical**: conceptualises political alignment as a multilevel configuration rather than a uniform relationship, and examines how these configurations interact with the institutional location of authority to produce different government responses.
- **Empirical**: extends GRID's cross-national analysis through subnational evidence (Colombia) and theoretically informed comparative investigation (Iraq, Burundi).
- **Overall**: specifies how the territorial configuration of political relationships and governmental authority conditions the form that protection and repression take.
