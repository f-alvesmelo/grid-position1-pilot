# Repression

Command: `/theory`. Sources: [[Project]], [[Measurement Research Design]]. RESEARCHER MATERIAL.

## Definition

The other pole of the government-response classification (alongside [[Protection]]), labeled **repressive-restrictive** in the researcher's coding scheme. Coded separately for de jure and de facto responses, within each policy domain.

- **De jure repressive/restrictive**: formal law, policy, regulation, or restriction that disadvantages, excludes, or constrains displaced populations.
- **De facto repressive/restrictive**: actual restriction, selective exclusion, withdrawal, delegation, or administrative/security barriers affecting displaced populations in practice, regardless of formal policy.

## A Third Category: No Meaningful Response

The response classification is **three-valued**, not binary: *protective / repressive-restrictive / no meaningful state response* (de facto) or *no formal response* (de jure). This matters methodologically: per the researcher's explicit **key rule** (see [[Coding Protocol]]), absence of a formal (de jure) policy must **not** automatically be coded as absence of a de facto response, and absence of a formal response must **not** automatically be treated as repression. Silence is not, by default, repression.

## Repression Is Domain- and Layer-Specific

As with protection, repression is coded per domain and per layer (de jure/de facto) — a government can be repressive in one domain while protective in another, and can be formally protective while de facto repressive (see the consistency matrix in [[Coding Protocol]]: "protective-inconsistent" and "repressive-inconsistent" cells).

## Theoretical Link (H2)

H2 states that **fragmented alignment** is associated with **more heterogeneous combinations of protective and repressive responses** — i.e., fragmentation is expected to produce *mixed* profiles (protective in some domains/layers, repressive in others) rather than uniformly more repression. Repression alone is not the predicted outcome of fragmentation; heterogeneity (protection *and* repression coexisting) is. See [[Research Questions]].
