# Protection

Command: `/theory`. Sources: [[Project]], [[Measurement Research Design]]. RESEARCHER MATERIAL.

## Definition

One pole of the government-response classification (the other being [[Repression]]). Protection is coded **separately for de jure and de facto responses**, within each policy domain.

- **De jure protective**: formal entitlement, programme, allocation, or institutional responsibility that favours displaced populations.
- **De facto protective**: actual provision, facilitation, or support delivered in practice, regardless of whether it is formalized.

## Protection Is Domain- and Layer-Specific

Protection is not a global property of a government. A government can be protective in one policy domain (e.g., humanitarian assistance) while repressive or restrictive in another (e.g., mobility and territorial control) — this is the entire point of treating response as multidimensional rather than scalar (see [[Concepts]]).

Within a domain, de jure and de facto protection can also diverge: a formally protective policy can go unimplemented, and de facto assistance can be provided even absent a formal entitlement. The **consistency matrix** in [[Coding Protocol]] captures this (consistent protection / protective-inconsistent / repressive-inconsistent / consistent repression-restriction).

## Pilot Domain: Humanitarian Assistance

For the pilot, "protective" in the humanitarian-assistance domain is defined as (per [[Measurement Research Design]] §3, §6):

- **De jure**: formal entitlement, programme, allocation, or responsibility that provides for humanitarian assistance.
- **De facto**: actual provision or facilitation of assistance.

## Theoretical Link (H1)

H1 states that **greater alignment** among national government, local authorities, and IDPs is associated with **more protective response profiles**. Protection is therefore the DV pole H1 predicts will increase with shared alignment; see [[Research Questions]].
