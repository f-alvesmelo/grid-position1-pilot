# Protection and Repression

Command: `/literature`. Sources: [[Project]]. See [[Literature Map]] for the full citation table.

## A Thin Literature Base — Flagged, Not Papered Over

Per the project text, the protection–repression continuum as a specific analytic frame is sourced to a **single citation**: Steele, Schwartz, & Lichtenheld (2024), the GRID project's own framing paper. No independently-authored literature specifically theorizing "protection" and "repression" as a continuum is cited elsewhere in [[Project]].

This is recorded here as an honest gap rather than filled with agent-invented citations (per AGENT.md's non-fabrication rule). See [[Research Gaps]].

## What the Existing Framing Establishes

Per [[Core Argument]] and [[Government Responses]]: government response to internal displacement is conceptualized along a continuum from protective to repressive, and — per this project's own extension — decomposed into:

- **Multiple policy domains**, not a single scalar response (see [[Concepts]]).
- **De jure and de facto layers** within each domain (see [[Protection]], [[Repression]]).

## Related Literature Providing Indirect Support

- Lichtenheld & Steele (2026) — the divergence between formal policy and informal practice, which underlies the de jure/de facto split (see [[Government Responses]]).
- Kalyvas (2006) — establishes that state/non-state behaviour toward populations under displacement-relevant conditions can be strategically produced, indirectly supporting the idea that "repression" is not simply an absence of capacity but can be a chosen response (see [[Internal Displacement]]).
