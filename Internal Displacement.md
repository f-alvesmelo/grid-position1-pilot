# Internal Displacement

Command: `/literature`. Sources: [[Project]]. See [[Literature Map]] for the full citation table.

## The Strategic-Displacement Strand

Kalyvas (2006) establishes that violence in civil war is strategically produced by combatants, not incidental — the basis for treating displacement itself as potentially strategic rather than a side effect of conflict. Steele (2011, 2017) extends this specifically to Colombia, showing displacement can be politically targeted ("political cleansing") and linked to democratic/electoral dynamics.

**Relevance**: this strand establishes that *who* gets displaced and *why* is already known to be politically shaped. This project's contribution is to shift attention from the politics of displacement's **production** to the politics of government **response** after displacement has occurred — an explicit, stated departure from Steele's own prior work (see [[Case Selection]]).

## Displacement as an Evolving Political Relationship

Tarkhanova (2023) frames internal displacement as "an evolving political relationship between citizens and the state, spanning strategic violence, contested legal citizenship, and localised institutional governance" (as quoted in [[Project]]). This is the project's opening conceptual frame.

**Relevance**: supports treating displacement not as a single event but as an ongoing relationship in which government response can shift over time and across governance levels — consistent with the project's multilevel, multi-domain theoretical architecture (see [[Core Argument]]).

## What Happens After Displacement

Lichtenheld & Steele (2025, 2026) shift the literature's attention toward what states do *after* displacement occurs: formal policy adoption, with implementation varying across contexts and domains, and informal practice diverging from formal commitment. The 2026 piece is the direct source of the project's de jure/de facto framing (see [[Protection]], [[Repression]]).

## Open Question for This Strand

None of these sources, per the project text, directly test whether the *political alignment* of the displaced population (as opposed to conflict dynamics or citizenship status) shapes the government response they receive. That is the specific gap this project's Position 1 pilot targets — see [[Research Gaps]].
