# Case Selection

Command: `/design`. Sources: [[Project]], [[Measurement Research Design]]. RESEARCHER MATERIAL.

## Cross-National Tier: Universe, Not Selection

The cross-national tier does not involve case *selection* in the usual sense — it covers **all countries in the finalized GRID dataset** and its substantive inclusion criteria (per [[Refined Measurement Framework]] and [[Measurement Research Design]] §7). Colombia, Burundi, and Iraq are explicitly **not** the complete country universe for this tier; they are the broader project's case-study contexts for the nested tiers below.

## Colombia — Theoretical + Data-Driven Selection

Selected as the **principal subnational case** (proposed candidate, not a fixed requirement) because:

- It allows linking individual-level victim registry data (Registro Único de Víctimas) with municipal electoral/political and conflict data — a data-driven feasibility rationale.
- Electoral turnover offers a plausible source of temporal variation for event-study analysis — a design rationale.
- It lets the project engage directly with, and differentiate itself from, existing Colombia scholarship: prior work (Steele, 2011; Steele, 2017) examined how political alignments contributed to the *production* of displacement; this project examines political determinants of government *responses after* displacement has occurred. This is a theoretical rationale — the case is chosen to isolate a different stage of the causal process than existing literature addresses, not merely because data exists.

## Iraq and Burundi — Theoretical (Nested) Selection

Selected as **complementary, asymmetric qualitative/mixed cases**, explicitly not a matched pair or representative sample:

- **Iraq**: location-level displacement/return data; variation in territorial/institutional authority — offers a setting to test whether the `authority_locus` mechanism (H3) operates similarly outside Colombia's institutional context.
- **Burundi**: a contrasting political context with more limited quantitative alignment measures, requiring greater reliance on qualitative process tracing — offers a hard case for the theory precisely because it cannot lean on the same quantitative infrastructure as Colombia.

The researcher explicitly frames this as following "the logic of nested analysis rather than treating data availability as a substitute for theoretical case selection" (Lieberman, 2005) — i.e., these cases are chosen to test whether statistically-identified mechanisms generalize across different institutional configurations, not because they happened to be convenient.

## Pilot Domain Selection: Humanitarian Assistance

Selected (researcher decision, 2026-09-27) as the first domain to operationalize because it is "directly within GRID's conceptualization of protective responses and provides a tractable setting in which formal commitments and actual practices can be distinguished" ([[Measurement Research Design]] §6). This is a **tractability** rationale, not a claim that humanitarian assistance is theoretically privileged over other domains.

## What Is Not Yet Decided

- No case-selection rule exists yet for which specific displacement episodes, within Colombia/Iraq/Burundi or within the cross-national universe, will be coded first for the pilot. ⚠ OPERATIONALIZATION OPEN.
- The researcher's memo explicitly recommends: "Use theoretically informed criteria and cross-national findings to identify the most informative setting for deeper investigation" ([[Measurement Research Design]] §8) — meaning case selection for the in-depth tier is meant to be informed by cross-national results, which do not yet exist.
